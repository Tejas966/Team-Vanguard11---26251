# 05 — Differentiators mapped to evaluation criteria

Criteria (novelty, feasibility, impact, clarity) come from public SIH guides. **I could not verify the official rubric or its weights.**
Competitor context comes from `01_landscape.md`. At least 7 public repos target SIH26251. The pattern most teams will submit is forecast line + Leaflet map + reroute toggle + always-fresh "IoT" + ledger. The strongest repo (RASAD) already shows P10/P90 forecasts and closure-aware planning.

## Chosen three (plus one cheap supporting feature)

| # | Differentiator | What the evaluator sees in the demo | Criteria | Effort | Risk | Evidence we show |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **Resilience test**: close a pass for N days and see who fails first, of what, and when | Drag a slider "Pass-1 closed: 10 days". Posts turn red in order. The ranked list reads "Post-H3 · fuel · stock-out day 6 (P = 0.72)". Then "Apply mitigation" (detour + air-drop by priority) and the failures move to later days. | Novelty, Impact, Clarity | M | M: the Monte Carlo must stay fast (target < 1 s for 8 posts × 4 classes × 500 paths) | Before/after table produced by code; test that closing an edge never *improves* a post's days of supply |
| **D2** | **Days of supply as a probability, not a number** | Each post × item shows "DoS 9 d (80% band 6–13) · P(stock-out before next convoy) 23%". Colour follows probability, with a text label as well (never colour alone). | Impact, Clarity | M | L | Fan chart; Monte Carlo seeded; unit tests on the stock-out probability (monotonic in stock and in demand) |
| **D3** | **Honest backtest panel** | One table per class: our model vs. naive / seasonal-naive / Croston-TSB. MASE, pinball loss, and "80% band contained the truth 78% of the time". Scored on held-out *mechanism B* data too. | Feasibility, Clarity | S–M | L | Table generated by `make backtest`; numbers in UI and slides read from the same JSON file |
| D4 *(supporting)* | **No-signal sensor handling** | One post's feed goes silent. The tile shows "No signal · last seen 31 h ago". The system falls back to forecast-only stock and its band **visibly widens**. | Feasibility, Clarity | S | L | Freshness states (Live / Stale / No signal) unit-tested; widened band computed, not hard-coded |

Why these: D1 answers the commander's real question ("if this road shuts, who runs out and when?"), and none of the repos we found show this consequence over time. D2 is what makes D1 believable. D3 answers "is the AI real?", which most teams cannot answer. D4 turns the IoT buzzword into a robustness feature for half a day of work.

## Considered and cut (for now)

| Candidate | Why not now | When |
|---|---|---|
| Explainable forecast drivers ("demand ↑18%: temp −8 °C, strength +40") | Easy to fake and hard to make look good in 90 s. LightGBM feature contributions (`pred_contrib=True`) make it cheap later. | Should (Day 4 if ahead) |
| What-if planner (add a sortie on day 4) | It is D1 with more inputs; build it on D1's engine. | Finale |
| Full VRP with time windows (OR-Tools) | Shortest path + greedy capacity is enough to show re-routing, and the interface allows swapping it in later. | Finale |
| Real OSM roads / real basemap | Conflicts with the no-real-locations rule; demo needs the network; borders are sensitive. | Won't |
| Blockchain ledger, chatbot, federated learning, LSTM | Every other team has these; they add risk and no evidence. | Won't |

## One-line pitch
*"Others show you a reroute. We show you who runs out, when, and how sure we are, and we prove the forecast is calibrated."*
